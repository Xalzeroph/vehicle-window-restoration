\section{Paired-Image Analysis and Supervision}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Image formation under camera-side occlusion}

Let $T$ denote the transmitted scene as recorded through tinted
glass and $R$ the reflection from the camera side. The tint and
its wavelength-dependent attenuation are included in $T$.
For pixel $x$ and color channel $k$, we write the observed
mixture as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_k(x)={}&(1-\eta_k(x))T_k(x)\\
&+(1-a(x))\beta_kR_k(x)\\
&+a(x)R_{b,k}(x)+C_{{\rm nl},k}(x),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
where $a(x)\in[0,1]$ describes camera-side occluder coverage,
$\eta_k$ represents local photometric changes between captures,
$R_b$ is light reflected by the occluder, and $C_{\rm nl}$
collects non-additive imaging effects. This formulation separates
the transmitted appearance, surface reflection, and changes
associated with the reference acquisition procedure.

The physical coverage region is
$\Omega=\{x:a(x)\geq\delta\}$. For training, we construct a
candidate region $\tilde\Omega$ from locally weak residual
responses in $I-gT$. Specifically, the proposed region is
obtained relative to a blockwise 90th-percentile reflection
envelope, followed by noise-floor and connected-component
filtering. Candidate regions are screened at the image-pair
level. The subsequent experiments test the value of their
spatial placement.

We measure local differences using the regional mean
$\bar X_{j,A}$ of image $X$ for pair $j$ and region $A$.
For $n=82$ screened pairs, the summary statistics are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q_T &=
\frac1n\sum_j
\frac{\bar T_{j,\tilde\Omega_j}}
     {\bar T_{j,\tilde\Omega_j^c}},\\
q_{I/T} &=
\frac1n\sum_j
\frac{\bar I_{j,\tilde\Omega_j}/\bar T_{j,\tilde\Omega_j}}
     {\bar I_{j,\tilde\Omega_j^c}/\bar T_{j,\tilde\Omega_j^c}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:regional-ratios}
\end{equation}
The measured values $q_T=0.963$ and $q_{I/T}=0.832$
describe different properties of the paired images:
reference brightness and the relative mixture-to-reference
intensity, respectively.

\subsection{Photometric scale and correction}

A correction estimated from strong reflections may remove
excessive intensity in regions where the reflection is weaker.
For a local unoccluded residual estimate $b_R$, define
\[
d_{\rm obs}=1-
\frac{\mathbb E[\hat R\mid\tilde\Omega]}
     {\mathbb E[b_R\mid\tilde\Omega]},
\qquad \hat R=I-gT.
\]
Under a proportional-correction model, the over-correction
ratio becomes
\begin{equation}
{\rm OCR}=
\frac{\mathbb E_{\tilde\Omega}[\hat T-I]}
     {\mathbb E_{\tilde\Omega}[T-I]}
\approx \frac{1}{1-d_{\rm obs}}.
\end{equation}
The regional estimator gives an OCR of $2.7\times$ on
the analyzed subset. This motivates the placement
experiments in Section~\ref{sec:diag}.

The simplified global model $I=T+R$ has a complementary
scale ambiguity. For an admissible scale $\lambda$,
\[
T'=\lambda T,\qquad R'=R+(1-\lambda)T
\]
leaves $I$ unchanged. Accordingly, the transmission ratio
\begin{equation}
\rho=\frac{\mu(T)}{\mu(I)}
=\frac{1}{1+\kappa},
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{\mu(R)}{\mu(T)}
\end{equation}
depends on how the mixture is decomposed. We measure
the associated photometric error using reference-fitted
affine corrections and direct interventions on a
conditioning parameter (Section~\ref{sec:photom}).

\subsection{Paired-residual-normalized region loss}

For a training mask $m_{\tilde\Omega}$, the mean paired
residual sets the loss scale:
\begin{equation}
D_{\tilde\Omega}=
\max\left(
\frac{\sum_xm_{\tilde\Omega}(x)|I(x)-T(x)|}
     {\sum_xm_{\tilde\Omega}(x)+\epsilon},
10^{-3}\right).
\end{equation}
The region reconstruction and one-sided correction terms are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L^{\rm err}_{\tilde\Omega}
&=
\frac{\sum_xm_{\tilde\Omega}(x)|\hat T(x)-T(x)|}
     {(\sum_xm_{\tilde\Omega}(x)+\epsilon)D_{\tilde\Omega}},\\
L^{\rm hinge}_{\tilde\Omega}
&=
\frac{\sum_xm_{\tilde\Omega}(x)
       [T(x)-\Delta(x)-\hat T(x)]_+}
     {(\sum_xm_{\tilde\Omega}(x)+\epsilon)D_{\tilde\Omega}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:region-loss}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta(x)=\max(4,0.5C_I(x))/255$.
The training objective adds
$L_{\tilde\Omega}=3L^{\rm err}_{\tilde\Omega}
+L^{\rm hinge}_{\tilde\Omega}$, averaged over eligible
training samples, to the common reconstruction objective.
The paired residual determines the normalization; the mask
determines its spatial support. Varying the latter isolates
the effect of region placement.

\subsection{Effect of spatial support on PSNR}

Suppose a correction acts on a fraction $f_B$ of image
pixels, where the initial mean squared error is $q_B$
times that of the remaining pixels. Eliminating all error
on this subset gives the direct improvement bound
\begin{equation}
\Delta{\rm PSNR}\leq
10\log_{10}
\frac{1+f_B(q_B-1)}{1-f_B}.
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
For a boundary occupying $f_B=0.28\%$ of the image
and $q_B=3$, the bound is $0.036$~dB. It quantifies
the impact of pixel changes confined to the boundary;
the empirical training experiment measures the broader
effect of applying a boundary loss.
