\section{Imaging Model and Supervision}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{A local mismatch in paired acquisition}

Let $T$ and $R$ denote the scene and camera-side reflection before
attenuation. In a linearized image domain, a useful model for pixel $x$
and color channel $c$ is
\begin{equation}
I_c(x)=\alpha_c(x)T_c(x)+\beta_cR_c(x)+C_{\mathrm{nl},c}(T,R),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_c(x)=\tau_c(1-\eta_c(x))$ contains the
glass transmittance $\tau_c$ and a local illumination change
$\eta_c(x)$. The residual $C_{\mathrm{nl}}$ accounts for departures
from additive layer formation. The intended target,
$T_{\mathrm{obs}}=\tau T$, retains the appearance induced by the
tinted glass while excluding the reflection.

Consider a camera-side object that obscures part of the reflected
light field. Writing $c(x)\in[0,1]$ for its local coverage gives
\begin{equation}
I_c(x)=\alpha_c(x)T_c(x)
 +(1-c(x))\beta_cR_c(x)+c(x)R_{b,c}(x)+C_{\mathrm{nl},c},
\label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
where $R_b$ represents light reflected by the occluder itself.
We define the \emph{occlusion region}
$\Omega_\delta=\{x:c(x)\geq\delta\}$ for a chosen threshold
$\delta$. This is a region in which the acquisition geometry changes;
it need not coincide with the support of visible reflected objects.
For the training images, we estimate candidate regions from dips in
the residual $\hat R=I-gT_{\mathrm{obs}}$ relative to a locally
interpolated upper envelope (blockwise 90th percentile), followed
by a noise-floor criterion and component-size filtering.
The resulting masks are acquisition-derived annotations rather than
single-image ground truth.

\subsection{Local over-correction}

A correction calibrated using the reflection strength outside
$\Omega$ can be too strong where camera-side coverage has attenuated
the reflection. Under a proportional-correction approximation, let
$\delta_{\mathrm{obs}}$ denote the local relative deficit,
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{obs}}
=1-\frac{\mathbb{E}[\hat R\mid\Omega]}
         {\mathbb{E}[b_R\mid\Omega]},
\]
where $b_R$ is the local unoccluded reflection estimate.
The ratio between an applied correction and the in-region correction
needed to match the paired target can then be expressed as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{OCR}
:=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{x\in\Omega}[\hat T-I]}
        {\mathbb{E}_{x\in\Omega}[T_{\mathrm{obs}}-I]}
\approx\frac{1}{1-\delta_{\mathrm{obs}}}.
\end{equation}
The approximation assumes a nonzero mean target correction and a
proportional global estimate. Our measured over-correction ratio is
$2.7\times$. This observation motivates testing spatially restricted
supervision; it does not imply that every restoration network applies
an explicit global correction.

\subsection{Ambiguity of the global transmission scale}

For the simplified mixture $I=T_{\mathrm{obs}}+R$, consider any
positive $\lambda$ for which the corresponding layers remain
admissible. The alternative decomposition
\[
T'_{\mathrm{obs}}=\lambda T_{\mathrm{obs}},\qquad
R'=R+(1-\lambda)T_{\mathrm{obs}}
\]
produces exactly the same $I$. Without an additional constraint on
$R$ or a photometric measurement, the observed image alone does
not uniquely specify the global scale
\begin{equation}
\rho=\frac{\mu(T_{\mathrm{obs}})}{\mu(I)}
     =\frac{1}{1+r},\qquad
r=\frac{\mu(R)}{\mu(T_{\mathrm{obs}})}.
\end{equation}
This construction establishes a non-identifiability in the model,
not the impossibility of predicting a likely scale from learned
image statistics. We quantify that distinction through measured
brightness bias and post-hoc photometric corrections
(Section~\ref{sec:photom}).

\subsection{Supervision objectives and spatial support}

The occlusion mask is used as a training-time signal, not an
additional input at test time. Writing $T$ for the paired training
target, a masked reflection-normalized reconstruction term and
a one-sided hinge term are
\begin{equation}
L^{\mathrm{err}}_\Omega
=\frac{\sum_xm_\Omega(x)|\hat T(x)-T(x)|}
       {\sum_xm_\Omega(x)|\hat R(x)|},\qquad
L^{\mathrm{hinge}}_\Omega
=\frac{\sum_xm_\Omega(x)[T(x)-\delta(x)-\hat T(x)]_+}
       {\sum_xm_\Omega(x)|\hat R(x)|}.
\end{equation}
The complete region objective is
$L_\Omega=\sum_cw_c(\lambda_\Omega L^{\mathrm{err}}_\Omega+
\lambda_hL^{\mathrm{hinge}}_\Omega)$, with
$\lambda_\Omega=3$ and $\lambda_h=1$. The denominator normalizes
the error by the estimated reflection magnitude in the selected
region. Our experiments distinguish this normalization from the
choice of the region itself.

Finally, consider the maximum \emph{direct} PSNR improvement
obtainable when a correction changes only a pixel subset occupying
fraction $f_B$. If its initial mean squared error is $r$ times
that of the remaining pixels, even removing all error in that
subset yields at most
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathrm{PSNR}\leq
10\log_{10}\frac{1+f_B(r-1)}{1-f_B}.
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
With $f_B\approx0.28\%$ and $r=3$, the limit is
$0.036$~dB. This bound concerns changes \emph{confined to the
subset}; it does not bound indirect, image-wide changes caused
by training with a boundary regularizer.
