\begin{abstract}
Paired images are widely used to supervise single-image reflection removal,
although a reference image may differ from its input in more than reflected
content. We study this issue for automotive tinted glass. A camera-side
occluder can alter both reflection and illumination, making a single global
photometric correction insufficient in some regions. On 82 image pairs,
candidate occlusion regions have a reference brightness ratio of $0.963$
relative to their surroundings and an inside/outside ratio of $0.832$ for
the normalized quantity $r=I/T$. These are image statistics, not direct
measurements of physical reflection or illumination losses. We also
describe the ambiguity of the global transmission scale under an
unconstrained single-image mixture model.

We investigate the consequences for supervision using 631 frame--reference
pairings from tinted-glass recordings and a frozen evaluation panel.
Fine-tuning on the best-matched training pool reaches $22.70$~dB,
compared with $20.50$~dB for the strongest evaluated public method,
and improves all 15 evaluation scenes. In a controlled three-seed
comparison, supervision on candidate occlusion regions yields
$19.41$~dB, whereas an area-matched random region reaches
$19.90$~dB. Labels with greater agreement to a residual-derived location
proxy likewise yield no restoration benefit when used for loss weighting.
These results support domain-matched paired training but do not
establish a distinct benefit from the proposed occlusion masks.
\end{abstract}
