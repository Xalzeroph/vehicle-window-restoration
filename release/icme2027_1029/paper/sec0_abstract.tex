\begin{abstract}
Single-image reflection removal relies on paired images, yet the way a
transmission reference is captured determines what the model learns.
We investigate this issue for automotive tinted glass, where covering
the camera-side surface changes the observed reflection and local
photometry. We analyze 631 frame--reference pairings and examine
three factors in supervised restoration: the training data distribution,
residual-normalized reconstruction, and spatial loss placement.
Measurements on 82 image pairs reveal systematic differences between
candidate occlusion regions and their surroundings, motivating a
controlled study with a fixed restoration backbone.

With domain-matched fine-tuning, RDNet reaches $22.70$~dB on a frozen
15-scene panel, compared with $20.50$~dB for the strongest evaluated
zero-shot method. In three-seed experiments, removing the region term
gives $17.76$~dB; using candidate occlusion masks gives $19.41$~dB,
while area-matched random regions reach $19.90$~dB. Reflection-location
supervision shows a similar distinction: a higher proxy IoU
($0.376$ versus $0.063$) accompanies a lower restoration PSNR when
the map weights the reconstruction loss. These results identify
data matching and the construction of reconstruction objectives as
central factors in tinted-glass reflection removal.
\end{abstract}
