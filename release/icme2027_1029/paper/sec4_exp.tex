\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Data and evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:data}

\textbf{Acquisition.}
We analyze 631 frame--reference pairings acquired through automotive
tinted glass across 13 recording scenes. Each scene contributes a
sequence of mixture frames and a transmission reference captured
with the camera-side reflection field covered by black velvet.
Consequently, multiple mixture frames may share a common target.
The median temporal separation between a frame and its reference
is 374 frames. We also study the unmodified recordings and three
digitally adjusted attenuation conditions ($8/11/14\%$). Table~
\ref{tab:data} summarizes the capture and evaluation protocol.

The primary evaluation uses a frozen 15-scene panel; photometric
diagnostics use a separately defined 12-scene subset. We report
PSNR and SSIM and include high-frequency and gradient statistics
where available. The primary result tables use the same panel
throughout. The paired-image acquisition provides candidate
camera-side occlusion regions that enter the training loss in
Section~\ref{sec:diag}.

\subsection{Zero-shot performance}
\label{sec:bench}

We first evaluate six publicly available reflection-removal
methods with their pretrained weights on the tinted-glass panel.
RRW obtains the highest PSNR at $20.50$~dB, followed by DSRNet
at $20.25$~dB and RDNet at $20.05$~dB. The identity
baseline scores $18.44$~dB. The range of performance, from
IBCLN's $18.12$~dB to RRW's $20.50$~dB, illustrates the
sensitivity of pretrained restoration models to the capture domain.

We next examine where the gains occur. The v13 evaluation partition
selects pixels with a reflection-related intensity criterion and
Otsu thresholding (median coverage $9.4\%$). Table~
\ref{tab:regiongain} reports each model's PSNR inside and
outside this partition, together with its gain over the
identity output on the same pixels.

The identity baseline scores $15.17$~dB inside the selected
region and $18.97$~dB outside it. RDNet gains $4.08$~dB

substantial part of the restoration improvement. The
candidate camera-side occlusion mask is a separate
training signal, examined through support-controlled
comparisons in Section~\ref{sec:diag}.

\subsection{Domain-matched fine-tuning}
\label{sec:ft}

($65.95$~M trainable parameters). The matched full-pool runs
use 6000 optimization steps, batch size 6, learning rate
$10^{-4}$, and the same real/synthetic mixture. We compare
two real-data compositions: apr+jun (170 pairs) and may+jun
(174 pairs), with three random seeds per setting.
Table~\ref{tab:ft} also includes earlier, single-seed
experiments with smaller training pools.

The may+jun setting reaches $22.70$~dB, improving on
the strongest evaluated zero-shot baseline ($20.50$~dB)
by $2.20$~dB. A separate paired evaluation export uses
$20.48$~dB for that baseline and reports a mean gain
of $2.22$~dB, with a scene-level $95\%$ interval
of $[+1.73,+2.72]$~dB. All 15 evaluation scenes
benefit from fine-tuning. The apr+jun pool reaches
$20.97$~dB under the matched recipe, giving a
$1.73$~dB difference between the training domains.

The paired-residual-normalized region term also has a
large effect. In matched ablations, removing it changes
apr+jun from $20.96$ to $17.77$~dB and may+jun
from $22.71$ to $18.33$~dB. The corresponding drops
of $3.19$ and $4.38$~dB establish the importance of
the regional objective in these training configurations.

A reference-fitted affine correction raises apr+jun
from $20.97$ to $22.13$~dB and may+jun from
$22.70$ to $23.14$~dB. The remaining $1.01$~dB
gap after affine fitting shows that the difference
between training pools extends beyond a global
brightness and contrast offset. An affine-invariant
training objective yields $20.46$ and $17.77$~dB
across two exploratory seeds, compared with
$20.96$~dB for its reference configuration.

\subsection{Where should the region loss act?}
\label{sec:diag}

We isolate the role of spatial support by keeping the
82-pair training set and optimization recipe fixed.
The first four arms in Table~\ref{tab:placement}
are averaged over seeds 101, 202, and 303.
The random masks occupy an area matched to the
candidate occlusion regions, either redrawn during
training or fixed for each pair.

Without the regional objective, mean PSNR is
$17.76$~dB. The candidate occlusion mask raises
it to $19.41$~dB, while a redrawn random disk
reaches $19.90$~dB and a per-pair fixed disk
reaches $20.25$~dB. Thus spatially normalized
training improves reconstruction, with the fixed
random support exceeding candidate occlusion
placement by $0.84$~dB in this experiment.

The candidate-mask arm also has the largest
seed standard deviation ($0.54$~dB), compared
with $0.16$~dB for redrawn masks and $0.29$~dB
for fixed masks. A single-seed whole-image
relative-error objective reaches $20.05$~dB.
These comparisons identify residual normalization
and support sampling as important variables for
the region loss, The regional objective has a larger
effect than choosing the candidate mask
over area-matched random supports.

A thin-boundary regularizer changes PSNR by
$+0.72$, $-0.37$, and $-0.17$~dB over three seeds,
averaging $+0.06$~dB. The direct-support bound
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound} gives $0.036$~dB for a
boundary covering $0.28\%$ of the image at three
times the background mean squared error. The
experiment measures the total training effect,
while the bound quantifies the contribution from
pixels within the boundary itself.

\subsection{Reflection-location supervision}
\label{sec:gate}

We next study a spatial cue with a different
meaning: the location of visible reflected content.
The gradient-comparison pseudo-label
$G_I>G_T$, following RRW~\cite{zhu2024rrw},
covers $48.1\%$ of the image. Against our
signed-residual-derived reflection-energy proxy,
its IoU is $0.063$. Training a location head
with the residual-derived label raises proxy
agreement to IoU $0.376$ (precision $0.739$,
recall $0.489$).

Higher proxy agreement and restoration quality
respond differently. When the resulting map
weights the reconstruction loss, the
residual-derived labels give $19.75$~dB,
versus $20.36$~dB for the gradient-comparison
labels. The $0.61$~dB reduction separates
location-proxy agreement from its utility as
a loss weight. A whole-image relative-error
objective reaches $20.05$~dB in its
separate screening run.

\subsection{Photometric calibration and control}
\label{sec:photom}

We quantify global photometric error by fitting
a scalar or affine intensity correction to each
prediction using its reference image. These
reference-fitted corrections measure the part of
the error attributable to global appearance.
Table~\ref{tab:photometric} gives the corresponding
scores for the April and May subsets.

In April, affine fitting raises identity to
$20.10$~dB and official RDNet to $20.40$~dB.
In May, the same procedure gives $25.77$ and
$25.44$~dB, respectively. Across 35 fine-tuned
arms evaluated on the 12-scene diagnostic subset,
the mean brightness bias is $6.66$ gray levels.
A per-image scalar correction
$g=\mu(T)/\mu(\hat T)$ increases PSNR by
$0.63$~dB on average, with a maximum
improvement of $1.89$~dB.

Finally, we measure the model's response to a
supplied photometric condition $c$ by holding
the image fixed and perturbing $c$:
\[
\mathrm{ControlGain}=
\frac{\partial\mu(\hat T)/\partial c}{\mu(I)}.
\]
The frozen conditioned model scores $0.020$;
an absorption-aware variant reaches $0.086$;
and counterfactual sampling of the condition
raises the response to $0.244$. The
uninformative-condition control scores $0.020$.
The latter comparison quantifies a $12.2\times$
increase in conditioning responsiveness.
It establishes photometric control as a
measurable property alongside reconstruction
accuracy.
