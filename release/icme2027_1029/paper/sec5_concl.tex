\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

We studied how paired supervision behaves when the reference image is
acquired through tinted automotive glass. Camera-side occlusion can change
both reflected content and illumination. The observed ratios
differ inside and outside proposed regions, although these
annotations do not isolate the two physical mechanisms.
The additive imaging model also leaves the absolute transmission
scale ambiguous without additional assumptions or measurements.

Our controlled experiments separate these issues from the benefits
of training on the target domain. Fine-tuning on the better-matched
data pool improves restoration on the frozen evaluation panel, but
the results do not establish a benefit from localizing the loss
to the proposed occlusion region: an area-matched random region
performs similarly across the tested seeds. Annotation noise
remains a plausible explanation. Reflection-location labels
with higher agreement to an image-derived proxy likewise
do not improve reconstruction when used only as loss weights. A support-area calculation clarifies the limited direct
effect of corrections restricted to a narrow boundary, while an
intervention tests whether explicit photometric conditions influence
the model output.

The broader implication is methodological. Improvements in paired
reflection removal should be attributed separately to data
distribution, loss normalization, spatial supervision, and
photometric control. Reporting the corresponding negative results
is necessary to determine which components actually account for
the observed gains.
