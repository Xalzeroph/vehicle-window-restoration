\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

We examined how the acquisition of paired references affects
supervised reflection removal through tinted automotive glass.
The analysis distinguishes camera-side occlusion, visible
reflection locations, and the global scale of the transmitted
scene. These factors have different meanings in the imaging
model and in the reconstruction objective.

The experiments show the importance of matching the training
domain: fine-tuned RDNet achieves $22.70$~dB on the frozen
15-scene panel, compared with $20.50$~dB for the strongest
evaluated zero-shot method. The paired-residual-normalized
region objective produces substantial gains, while
area-matched random supports outperform the candidate
occlusion masks in the matched training runs.
Likewise, improved agreement with a reflection-location
proxy accompanies lower restoration accuracy when the
map is used as a loss weight. Photometric corrections
and conditioning interventions further separate global
appearance from spatial reconstruction.

These findings provide an experimental basis for designing
paired supervision: select the training distribution
carefully, evaluate normalization independently from
region placement, and measure spatial cues by their
effect on the restored image.
